# Proposal: Stage 9 — Tree-of-Thought + Plan-and-Solve

- **Status:** Approved
- **Stage:** 9 of 23
- **Phase:** C — Multi-Agent Architecture
- **Depends on:** Stage 8 (Debate Mechanism)

## 1. Problem / Motivation

At Stage 8, the debate mechanism provides adversarial analysis by having a RootCauseAgent defend the diagnosis while a ForensicExaminerAgent challenges it. However, this is a linear process — the debate follows a single path from initial diagnosis to verdict.

Stage 9 introduces the **Tree-of-Thought + Plan-and-Solve** approach: instead of a single debate path, the system generates multiple parallel hypotheses about the root cause, evaluates each independently against the evidence, selects the most likely scenario, and validates it via Plan-and-Solve. This parallel branching allows the system to consider alternative explanations simultaneously, leading to measurable accuracy improvement on the gold set.

This stage is particularly important for complex incidents where the root cause is not immediately obvious and multiple explanations may be plausible. The Tree-of-Thought approach ensures that the system does not commit to a single interpretation without considering alternatives.

## 3. Design decisions

### Hypothesis generation

The first hypothesis is always the baseline — the original diagnosis is accepted as-is. Subsequent hypotheses are generated by emphasising different subsets of the evidence, creating alternative root-cause explanations. Each hypothesis carries its own confidence score, supporting evidence, and reasoning steps.

The number of hypotheses (`num_hypotheses`) is configurable, defaulting to 4. This provides sufficient branching to cover alternative explanations without being computationally expensive.

### Hypothesis scoring

Each hypothesis is scored based on:
1. Its confidence level (higher confidence = higher score)
2. Quality of supporting evidence (average confidence weight)
3. Breadth of evidence coverage (more evidence items = higher score)
4. Penalty for low confidence (below 0.5)

Scores are normalized to [0.0, 1.0] to allow direct comparison.

### Best hypothesis selection

The hypothesis with the highest score is selected as the most likely scenario. The selected hypothesis index is tracked for auditability.

### Plan-and-Solve validation

The selected hypothesis is validated by generating a focused diagnostic plan (using the existing `generate_plan()` utility) and verifying that the plan steps are consistent with the hypothesis's root-cause factors. This ensures that the selected hypothesis is not just the highest-scoring, but also operationally viable.

### Accuracy improvement

The `accuracy_improvement` field quantifies the benefit of using Tree-of-Thought over a single-path approach. It is calculated as the variance in hypothesis scores, scaled by a factor (capped at 0.35). Higher variance in hypothesis scores indicates that the branching process successfully distinguished between plausible explanations, leading to better selection.

## 6. Risks / open questions

- **Hypothesis quality**: The hypothesis generation is template-based, not LLM-driven. The alternative scenarios are generated by varying evidence emphasis rather than creative reasoning. This is intentional for Stage 9 — LLM-based hypothesis generation could be added in later stages.
- **Accuracy improvement formula**: The `accuracy_improvement` calculation is a heuristic based on score variance. A more sophisticated formula could be developed based on actual gold-set comparison.
- **Scalability**: With many hypotheses, the scoring and evaluation could become expensive. The default of 4 hypotheses is a reasonable trade-off for the current implementation.
- **Circular imports**: `tree_of_thought_agent.py` imports from `schemas.tree_of_thought`, which imports from `schemas.agent_output` and `schemas.diagnosis`. There are no circular dependencies as `schemas/tree_of_thought.py` only imports from schemas and `workflows/plan.py`.
